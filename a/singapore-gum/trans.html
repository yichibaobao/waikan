1A sticky situation: why Singapore still won't sell you chewing gum

2More than three decades after it banned the stuff, the city-state shows little sign of relenting.

3Visitors find the rule baffling; many locals barely notice it.

4In 1992 Singapore made it illegal to import or sell chewing gum, a law that still raises eyebrows abroad.

5The government's reasoning was practical rather than moral.

6Cleaners were spending a fortune scraping gum off pavements, stairwells and lift buttons.

7The last straw, according to most accounts, came when vandals stuck gum on the door sensors of the new metro trains, causing doors to jam and services to stall.

8Singapore had a reputation to protect.

9Since independence it has prided itself on being clean, green and orderly, and its leaders have never been shy about regulating daily life to keep it that way.

10Fines for littering, spitting and failing to flush public toilets have long been part of the deal.

11To outsiders, such rules can look like a nanny state at its most fussy.

12Many residents are unbothered.

13They point out that the streets are spotless and the trains run smoothly, and that nobody is being asked to sacrifice more than a minor pleasure.

14Since 2004, under a trade deal with America, pharmacies have been allowed to sell gum with health benefits, such as nicotine gum for smokers trying to quit.

15Buyers were at first required to leave their names with the pharmacist.

16A persistent myth holds that chewing gum in Singapore can get you caned.

17It cannot; the famous caning of an American teenager in 1994 was for vandalising cars, not for gum.

18But the myth survives because it fits a wider story about a strict little country that likes to keep order.

19Singaporeans, for their part, tend to treat the ban with amused indifference.

20Some joke that the first thing they buy on holiday abroad is a packet of gum.

21Others say they have simply lost the habit.

22Either way, the gum has not come back, and neither has the mess.

23In Singapore, at least, some problems stay solved because nobody is allowed to chew on them.
