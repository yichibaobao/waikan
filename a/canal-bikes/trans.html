1Gone fishing, for bicycles: the strange catch of Amsterdam's canals

2Every year a small crane boat hauls thousands of bikes out of the city's waterways.

3The catch says a lot about a country where bicycles outnumber people.

4On a grey weekday morning, a flat boat glides along a canal in central Amsterdam, its crane arm dipping into the murky water.

5Moments later it surfaces with a dripping tangle of metal: handlebars, wheels and a bike lock still firmly fastened.

6The crew pile the wreckage onto the deck and move on to the next spot.

7By some estimates, they pull out between 12,000 and 15,000 bicycles a year.

8How so many bikes end up in the water is a matter of some debate.

9Some are stolen, ridden around and then dumped; others are pushed in by pranksters after a night out.

10A few slip in by accident when they are parked too close to the edge, and some owners, tired of an old wreck, simply leave theirs to the water.

11Locals joke that there are three layers at the bottom of each canal: mud, bikes and then more mud.

12The scale of cycling in the Netherlands helps explain the problem.

13The country has an estimated 23 million bicycles for 18 million people, and in cities many residents own two: a good one for long rides and a battered one for leaving at the station.

14The second kind is rarely locked with much care, which is precisely what makes it a target.

15Parking is a constant headache.

16Utrecht's main station now has a multi-storey garage with room for 12,500 bikes, reputedly the largest in the world, and it still fills up on busy days.

17Little of what comes out of the water is thrown away.

18The bikes are taken to a depot, where the few that are registered can be traced back to their owners.

19The rest are scrapped and the metal is recycled, sometimes ending up in new bicycles.

20Seen this way, the canal fishermen are not so much cleaning up a mess as closing a loop.

21Visitors often stop to film the crane at work, delighted by such an odd spectacle.

22For residents it is merely routine, as unremarkable as rubbish collection.

23Yet the sight of a bicycle rising from a canal says something about the Dutch: practical, unsentimental and quietly amused by their own habits.

24In a city built on water and wheels, it was only a matter of time before the two met.
