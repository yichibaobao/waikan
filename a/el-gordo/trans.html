1The ticket you cannot afford to skip: inside Spain's Christmas lottery

2Every December, millions of Spaniards buy into the same few numbers, hoping for the prize they call El Gordo, the Fat One.

3Few expect to win; what they really fear is being left out.

4The draw, held each year on 22 December, dates back more than two centuries and is broadcast live for hours.

5Children from a Madrid school sing out the winning numbers and prizes in a distinctive chant that Spaniards recognise instantly.

6Full tickets are expensive, so most people buy a fraction of one, known as a décimo, a tenth.

7Many go further and split tickets with colleagues, neighbours, the local bar or the football club.

8This habit of clubbing together is the heart of the tradition.

9If an office shares a number and it comes up, half the staff may resign the next morning.

10If it comes up and you did not chip in, you may spend years being reminded of it.

11In 2011 the top prize fell on a single village in Aragon, where almost every household had bought a share of the same number.

12One man, it is said, had not; the cameras found him, cheerful but rueful, among his newly rich neighbours.

13The odds of winning the top prize are slim, roughly one in 100,000 per number.

14But the lottery spreads its money widely, paying out thousands of smaller prizes, and even a refund for tickets ending in the same digit as the top prize.

15Economists grumble that it is a tax on hope.

16Yet few Spaniards seem to mind: for many, the ticket is less a bet than a ritual, like a card sent to an old friend.

17No sooner has the last prize been sung than winners in lucky towns are spraying each other with sparkling wine on national television.

18Shops that sold the winning number hang banners boasting of their luck, and the queues outside them grow longer the following year.

19Superstition plays its part, too.

20People travel across the country to buy from outlets with a lucky reputation, and some rub their tickets on a pregnant friend's belly for luck.

21For all the talk of jackpots, the real prize may be the feeling of being in it together.

22In a year of rising prices, a shared ticket is a small, cheap promise that nobody will be forgotten.

23Most will lose, of course.

24But they will lose together, and that, Spaniards insist, is half the fun.
