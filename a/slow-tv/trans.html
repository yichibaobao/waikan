1Nothing happens, beautifully: how Norway turned boredom into a television hit

2A train ride, a boat trip and a log fire have all drawn huge audiences, despite having no plot whatsoever.

3Their success suggests that viewers are hungrier for calm than broadcasters had assumed.

4In 2009 Norway's public broadcaster, NRK, filmed the seven-hour train journey from Bergen to Oslo and showed it in full.

5There was no presenter, no music and hardly any editing to speak of; just the view from the front of the train, tunnels included.

6Producers expected a few curious viewers; instead, more than a million Norwegians tuned in at some point.

7Encouraged, they went further.

8A later broadcast followed a coastal ferry for more than five days, live and without a break, as it sailed up the country's jagged coastline.

9Crowds gathered at harbours along the route to wave at the cameras, and some viewers admitted they had not gone to bed for days.

10Then came a twelve-hour programme about firewood, much of it showing a fire slowly burning in a hearth.

11Viewers complained, half-seriously, about how the logs had been stacked.

12Critics who predicted the format would quickly wear thin have been proved wrong.

13Slow TV, as it came to be known, has since been copied by broadcasters in several other countries.

14Part of the appeal is practical: it makes ideal background viewing, something to glance at while cooking or knitting.

15But many fans say it offers something rarer: permission to slow down.

16Modern media are designed to grab attention and hold on to it, with cliffhangers, notifications and endless scrolling.

17Slow TV does the opposite.

18It asks nothing of the viewer and rewards patience with small surprises: a moose by the tracks, a wave from a stranger on a bridge.

19Some psychologists argue that this kind of gentle attention is restorative, much like a walk in a park.

20There is also a quieter, national pride at work.

21Norwegians often describe their landscape as part of who they are, and the programmes let them admire it from the sofa.

22Outsiders may find the idea of watching knitting for an entire night hard to fathom.

23Yet the ratings speak for themselves.

24In a world that never stops shouting, a programme that simply whispers can be oddly gripping.

25Sometimes the most exciting thing on television is nothing at all.
