1Mind the elves: why Iceland's roads sometimes take the long way round

2In a country of volcanoes and hot springs, some building projects are slowed by creatures nobody has ever seen.

3The reasons say less about magic than about how Icelanders treat their land.

4Picture a road crew on the edge of Reykjavik, ready to blast through a field of mossy lava.

5Before the first charge is set, a group of protesters turns up and asks them to stop.

6Their worry is not the view or the birds, but a large rock that, according to local lore, is home to hidden people.

7Stories of the huldufólk, or hidden folk, have been passed down in Iceland for centuries.

8They are said to look much like humans, to live inside rocks and hills, and to take offence when their homes are disturbed.

9Few Icelanders will swear that elves exist, yet surprisingly few will rule it out.

10Asked in surveys, many prefer a cautious shrug: better safe than sorry.

11Over the years, that caution has shaped real decisions.

12Roads have been rerouted to spare elf rocks, and builders have occasionally hired mediators who claim to negotiate with the hidden residents.

13When machines break down or workers fall ill on a site, someone usually jokes that the elves are behind it, and only half of them are joking.

14In one well-known case, a boulder was lifted out of a road's path and set down a safe distance away, by agreement with all sides.

15Sceptics roll their eyes at such stories.

16But the elves often serve as a stand-in for something easier to defend: a deep respect for the landscape.

17Iceland's lava fields took thousands of years to form, and once bulldozed they cannot be put back.

18Protesters who invoke the hidden folk are often the same people who campaign for nature reserves.

19Tourism has given the legends a second life.

20Visitors can take guided walks through “elf gardens”, buy maps of known dwellings and even stay in hotels that keep a small house outside for their invisible neighbours.

21Locals are happy to play along, partly because it brings in money and also because they enjoy the joke.

22Whether or not anyone believes in them, the hidden folk do a useful job.

23They remind a fast-growing country to pause before it digs, and to ask what might be lost.

24Plenty of places could do with a few elves of their own.
