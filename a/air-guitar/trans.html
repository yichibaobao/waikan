1Strumming thin air: why grown-ups fly to Finland to play guitars that do not exist

2Every summer, a small city near the Arctic Circle hosts a contest in which the instruments are invisible and the stakes are, by design, very low.

3Yet the event takes its silliness very seriously, and that may be the secret of its charm.

4Picture a stage, a roaring crowd and a performer in leather trousers, eyes shut, fingers flying across a guitar that is not there.

5For 60 seconds he leaps, kneels and pulls faces to a song he did not choose, then bows as if he had just played a sold-out stadium.

6This is the Air Guitar World Championships, held in Oulu, Finland, since 1996.

7What began as a joke at a local music festival has grown into an international fixture, with national champions from dozens of countries competing for the title.

8The rules are short and strict.

9Contestants may not touch a real instrument, nor may they rely on backing musicians; the guitar must be entirely imaginary.

10Judges score each performance on technical skill, stage presence and a hard-to-define quality the organisers call 'airness'.

11A good air guitarist, in other words, must convince the audience that the music is coming from his fingers, not the speakers.

12Mistakes are allowed, but only if they are made with conviction.

13To outsiders, it can look like an elaborate excuse to dress up and show off.

14Competitors beg to differ.

15Many have practised for months in front of a mirror, studying the moves of rock legends frame by frame.

16Some insist that air guitar is a genuine art form, one that demands timing, stamina and a total lack of embarrassment.

17After a single minute on stage, several admit, they are as out of breath as any athlete.

18The festival also wears a cheerful philosophy on its sleeve.

19Its organisers like to joke that you cannot hold a weapon while playing air guitar, so if everyone took it up, wars might simply stop.

20Nobody pretends this is a serious peace plan, but the slogan captures the spirit of the thing: harmless, generous and slightly absurd.

21There is something refreshing about a contest in which no one can be accused of having an unfair advantage.

22No expensive equipment is needed, and a child with enough nerve can, in principle, beat a professional musician.

23In an age when so much of life is measured, ranked and sold, a world title for doing nothing at all is a small act of rebellion.

24And unlike most championships, this one leaves its fans with nothing worse than a sore throat.

25The guitars may be imaginary; the joy, judging by the crowds, is anything but.
